# Multisport terminal-reversal audit v2

## Status

Complete. This is an exploratory descriptive audit of the endpoint behavior in
the nine-sport FLB-over-time report. It does not change the published estimator.

## Research contract

- Question: why does the bought-contract D10-minus-D1 calibration spread turn
  upward near the recorded event end, and why do some endpoint intervals narrow?
- Observation: exact resolved moneyline BUY fill.
- Calibration: eventual bought-contract outcome minus purchase price, `Y - P`.
- Tails: fixed bought-price D1 and D10 bins.
- Primary diagnostic: direct decompositions in `[.80,.90)`, `[.90,.95)`,
  `[.95,.99)`, `[.99,1]`, and `[.999,1]`, plus nested final 30 to 600 second
  windows.
- Samples: filtered trades; the same interior price support with all buyers; and
  all available `0 < P < 1` trades.
- Time: exact block timestamp normalized by the accepted recorded start and end.
  Trades after the recorded end are excluded.

## Findings

The plotted point at `T=1` is not a final-one-percent estimate. The live kernel
has bandwidth `.10`, so the endpoint uses the one-sided final tenth `(0.9,1]`,
weighted toward the end. The simple final-tenth bins reproduce the sign pattern,
so the reversal is not created by the kernel alone.

The literal filtered final-one-percent spreads are 9.81 pp ATP, 8.15 pp CBB,
10.45 pp CFB, 6.77 pp EPL, 7.69 pp MLB, 2.85 pp NBA, 8.67 pp NFL, 6.51 pp NHL,
and 4.98 pp WNBA. CFB and WNBA fail the project's 500-fill-per-tail display
floor in this narrow window and should be treated as withheld.

The immediate mechanism is outcome polarization. In the final one percent, D1
win rates are 0 to 4.4 percent and D10 win rates are 97.0 to 100 percent. Mean
filtered prices remain around 4 to 5 cents in D1 and 95 to 96 cents in D10.
Consequently D1 calibration becomes negative and D10 calibration positive.
Across sports, 94.8 to 100 percent of events with both tails have a positive
within-event spread, so the result is not generated by a few games.

Within the same interior price support, including flagged buyers changes the
final-one-percent spread by at most 0.64 pp across sports. Buyer filtering is
therefore not the main mechanism. Restoring boundary-near trades reduces the
spread by 1.61 to 4.76 pp outside MLB. The MLB exact source has no additional
boundary-price observations, so that comparison is unavailable there.

The endpoint intervals narrow because terminal calibration errors become much
less dispersed while substantial clustered support remains. For example,
filtered ATP D1 calibration standard deviation falls from 17.90 pp in
`[.80,.90)` to 4.23 pp in `[.99,1]`; ATP D10 falls from 21.54 to 5.27 pp.
Comparable reductions occur in CBB, CFB, MLB, NFL, and NHL. The endpoint kernel
still contains hundreds or thousands of events, depending on sport. The common
sport-panel vertical scale also makes the narrow bands look visually smaller
than they are.

Exact recorded-end trades are only 0 to 3.8 percent of filtered final-one-percent
tails. The result is therefore not driven by a pile of fills timestamped exactly
at the endpoint. It remains possible for the economic outcome to be effectively
known before the provider's final recorded event timestamp; that is part of the
terminal-state interpretation rather than post-end trading.
